What is the difference between public and private IP?

Your public IP is the address visible outside your home or office network. A private address, such as 192.168.1.10, identifies a device inside that network. This page shows public addresses, not your device's local Wi-Fi address.

Why do I have both IPv4 and IPv6?

IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses, usually written as four numbers separated by dots. IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses written in hexadecimal with colons. Networks that support both can use either, so this page checks each version separately.

Why wasn't my IPv6 address detected?

A failed IPv6 check can mean your network has no working IPv6 route. It can also be caused by a VPN, proxy, firewall, DNS filtering, or a temporary endpoint failure. It does not prove that your device or provider cannot support IPv6. Try refreshing or checking another network.

Does this show my VPN's IP address?

If your VPN or proxy carries the request, the endpoint sees its public exit address. IPv4 and IPv6 can follow different routes, so you may see different network providers or locations. This page reports the two observed connections and is not a complete VPN leak test.

How accurate are the provider and location?

Network and location details come from Cloudflare's IP metadata. The organization may be your ISP, mobile carrier, VPN provider, or another network owner. The city is an estimate and may describe a provider's gateway rather than your location. No GPS or browser location permission is used.

Can my public IP address change?

Yes. Changing networks, reconnecting your router, switching a VPN, or your provider's address allocation can change it. On networks with shared public IPv4, several customers may have the same address. Use Refresh to check again.
